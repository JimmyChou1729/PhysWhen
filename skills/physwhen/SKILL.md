Use the user's scientific material, requested length and intended reader as the authority. PhysWhen combines exploratory whole-paper organization with local prose operations. Historical examples establish possible organizations, not ideal human writing or a proven readability benefit.

## Editorial scope and scientific invariants

This is a writing and structural-editing skill, not permission to referee, repair, extend, or validate the science. Retrieval informs terminology, exposition and organization; it does not authorize replacing the author's assumptions or conclusions with those of a retrieved paper. Preserve scientific commitments in both directions: do not strengthen or weaken claims, add or remove hypotheses, alter equations, definitions, proof steps, data, numerical results, uncertainty, novelty scope or substantive citation support. An apparently reasonable scientific correction still requires separate explicit authorization. Put suspected scientific errors, missing support or conflicting sources in a separate note; do not apply them in the manuscript, even with a change log, under an editing-only request.

For a new draft, express the user's supplied or explicitly agreed scientific content. A topic-only request can support a clearly identified literature explanation or outline, not invented original results. Do not undertake new research or fill a proof gap merely to complete the writing. Terminology changes must preserve meaning; if equivalence is uncertain, retain the term and explain the ambiguity separately.

## Handle a simple request

Accept short requests such as “Use physwhen to revise this paper” or “Use physwhen to write a paper from these notes.” Read the supplied draft or notes and infer the relevant scope; do not make the user restate these instructions or complete an intake checklist. Use the existing draft's audience and length as defaults; for a new draft infer a reasonable editorial scope from the material and state consequential defaults such as audience and length briefly; do not infer new scientific assumptions. Write the paper in English unless another language is requested.

A broad revision request requires a structural assessment and permits useful changes to argument order and prose while preserving the supplied science; an explicitly local edit stays local. Return the requested draft or revision with a brief account of actual structural changes; call out material gaps or scientific conflicts separately. If only a topic is supplied, ask for the missing scientific results or offer a clearly labeled outline; do not invent original findings to produce a complete-looking research paper. Ask only about missing information that materially affects scientific content or the requested outcome.

## Choose the scope

Complete the evidence workflow below for both drafting and revision. For a copy edit or isolated paragraph, keep retrieval focused and apply only the relevant local operations below. For a whole draft, outline or authorized structural revision, inspect the supplied question, results, evidence and necessary conditions before choosing sections. Respect a journal's required organization. Do not turn a short request into a full-paper planning exercise.

## Assess the whole-paper structure before polishing

For any full-paper drafting or revision request, first map the existing question, assumptions, definitions, main result, supporting arguments and interpretation. Compare the current reading route with plausible alternatives. Decide whether reordering, merging, splitting or retitling sections, moving existing paragraphs or proof blocks, or relocating technical detail would materially help the intended reader. A general request to improve the paper authorizes such editorial reorganization unless the user restricts it; do not require a separate magic phrase asking for structure.

When reorganization is justified, carry it out before sentence polishing. Preserve each scientific unit and its conditions, explicit dependencies, necessary intermediate steps and citation attachments; repair numbering and cross-references. A result-first preview may precede its proof when clearly marked, without changing what establishes what. New signposting may restate supplied relationships but must not invent an argument. Keep an internal old-to-new content map and check that reorganization loses no scientific material. Existing equations and proof steps may move or be renumbered, not be scientifically rewritten.

Do not change section order merely to demonstrate activity. If the current route is suitable, keep it and state the structural assessment briefly. Renaming headings or adding introductory sentences alone is not a whole-paper reorganization; describe the actual level of editing honestly. For a deliberately local edit, assess only its local structure.

## Retrieve, establish support, write, and check

Before writing or revising any scientific passage, including a short edit, read and follow [references/evidence-workflow.md](references/evidence-workflow.md). Search relevant literature and terminology, open the sources needed for the task, and assemble concise claim–evidence notes before drafting. For revision, anchor those notes to the author's existing scientific commitments; outside literature is editorial reference, not replacement scientific content. The historical style corpus does not replace task-relevant retrieval. Scale the search to the passage or paper; do not expand a local edit into an exhaustive review.

Use those notes to constrain the draft. After drafting, check the final claims, terminology, citations, equations and conditions against the actual evidence and supplied material; remove scientific additions introduced by the editor, and disclose concerns about the original material separately without changing its claims. This is an editorial fidelity check, not scientific certification. Give a brief source/check summary outside the paper, with substantive gaps, without exposing a lengthy planning narrative.

If retrieval is unavailable or the user explicitly disallows it, say that the required literature check was not performed. Continue only with clearly identified source-limited editing or a provisional outline where appropriate; do not invent sources, claim verification, or fabricate research results. This workflow is a user-requested design constraint, not an empirically demonstrated benefit of the 500-paper corpus.

## Organize the argument when needed

**G1 — Align the paper's promise with what it establishes.** Identify the central question or comparison and the supplied claims that answer it. Distinguish a result from a proposed mechanism, a consistency check and an open problem. An introduction can state the answer early; its promise must retain the supplied restrictions. At the end, return to the question without changing the author’s scientific claim strength. A partial answer or explicitly open question can be the correct ending. Skip another summary when the final analysis already performs this job.

**G2 — Organize around dependencies and branches.** Work out which assumptions, definitions, methods or earlier results are needed for the central claims. Use this to choose a reading route, not to impose one section per item. A result-first overview followed by derivation is legitimate; so is a derivation-led route. If regimes or mechanisms have different assumptions or conclusions, give the reader a recognizable branch point and compare them where their relation matters. Keep conditions recoverable at the claim, including when support appears later. Do not invent a missing proof or use a transition word to conceal it. If supplied scientific information is missing, identify the gap separately; do not narrow the author’s claim or supply a missing step without explicit scientific-edit authorization. Use placeholders only when explicitly requested.

**G3 — Distinguish preview, development and return.** A preview says what the reader is about to learn; the development supplies the actual reasoning or evidence; a later return should help compare, interpret or delimit it. Consolidate repetition that adds none of these functions. Do not remove a useful recap merely because its claim appeared before. Section titles, paragraph counts and a uniform introduction–methods–results template cannot substitute for this decision.

**G4 — Allocate space to the reasoning that matters.** Keep the main claim’s assumptions and logical bridge recoverable on the main reading path. Give a nontrivial transition enough explanation; do not expand an already transparent algebraic step to meet a prose quota. A full technical proof, including one central to the result, may be placed in an appendix when scope allows: retain its role, principal steps, result and necessary conditions in the body, with an accurate pointer. Routine auxiliary derivations can also be grouped or deferred. Never hide a missing argument in an appendix or claim that an unwritten appendix contains proof. Section length need not be equal. An existing discussion can contain a new calculation; its heading alone does not make it a recap.

For an unfamiliar organizational choice, consult [references/structure-evidence.md](references/structure-evidence.md). It describes observed routes and counterexamples with source anchors. These four operations are candidate design choices, not four empirically established improvements.

## Keep local passages useful

- Give a nontrivial display a task if its purpose is otherwise unclear. Skip extra lead-ins for short definitions, already motivated relations and transparent consecutive algebra.
- Where needed, make a central result usable with a supplied consequence, physical reading, subsequent use or scope statement. Do not translate the equation mechanically into a redundant sentence. Several equations can share an explanation.
- Keep the actual condition with the claim it limits. Preserve a simulation's domain, approximation or conditional bound; do not replace it with vague hedging or add uncertainty to imitate a style.

Motivation connects the supplied problem to its question; setup makes needed variables and assumptions recoverable; derivation preserves the dependency; interpretation preserves scope. Mixed paragraphs are legitimate. The earlier local evidence and its separate denominators are summarized in [references/local-evidence.md](references/local-evidence.md).

## Make terminology and sentences carry a definite meaning

Prefer terms established in the supplied material or relevant physics literature. Do not coin technical-sounding labels, compound nouns, or synonyms merely for variety. Keep established terminology and notation consistent; unfamiliarity, hyphenation, or absence from this project's corpus is not evidence that a term is invalid.

For a suspect expression, check its definition and use in the supplied material first. Consult relevant sources when needed and available; if its meaning remains unclear, flag that uncertainty rather than declaring it nonexistent, inventing a definition, or silently replacing it with a different physical concept. Preserve a genuinely new author-defined term with its definition and scope; make a new label only when it serves a distinct concept and is explicitly introduced. Explain necessary technical terms at first relevant use only to the extent the intended reader needs.

Check the proposition, not just individual words: can the reader identify the physical object, the relation or operation, and the conditions that matter? Replace decorative noun clusters and vague verbs with the concrete relation supported by the supplied equation or argument. If no such relation is supported, identify the missing meaning separately instead of making a fluent scientific assertion. Do not strengthen association into causation, an approximation into equality, or a diagnostic into a mechanism while simplifying. Avoid synonym swapping, universal word bans, compulsory glossaries, and blanket removal of specialist vocabulary.

This terminology check responds to a user-reported failure mode. It is an editorial design constraint, not a finding measured in the historical corpus or a demonstrated readability improvement.

## Preserve the scientific contract

Preserve supplied equations, signs, units, numerical values, variable meanings, negations, necessary conditions and quantifier scope unless a scientific change is requested. Identify conflicting supplied statements rather than silently choosing one. Use authorized citations; do not invent novelty claims, literature gaps, results or source quotations. Author-presented support in an example is not independent validation of its physics.

Return the requested prose or outline. Keep the planning ledger internal unless requested or needed to expose a material gap. The optional [scripts/check_outline.py](scripts/check_outline.py) checks a supplied outline ledger's references and coverage, not the validity of an argument. Its JSON contract and invocation are in [references/outline-contract.md](references/outline-contract.md). No formal JSON outline ledger is required for ordinary writing; the concise claim–evidence notes in the evidence workflow are still required.

Do not tune a short excerpt to a whole-paper median. Never output an AI-authorship probability, a single human-likeness score or a claim of improved understanding merely because an organization occurs in historical papers.
